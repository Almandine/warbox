Once the data is in a spreadsheet, it is one copy-paste away from being readable
by something other than me. So I tried it: paste the order of battle, describe the
scenario in two sentences, and ask for a plan.

## Where it helped

Summarising is the obvious win. "Which formations arrive after turn 20, and what
is their combined strength" is a question the sheet can answer, but not in one
line, and not while I am thinking about something else.

It was also good at noticing things I had stopped seeing — an artillery battalion
sitting in the arrival list that I had never once used.

## Where it did not

Anything spatial. Without the map it has no idea that two units three hexes apart
may as well be in different countries, and it will happily propose a
concentration that would take six turns to achieve.

It is also confident when it is wrong, which is worse than being wrong quietly.
Every number it gives back is worth checking against the sheet it was given.

## Worth repeating?

For reading and summarising, yes. For deciding, no. Which is roughly what the
companions are for anyway: they do not tell you what to do, they just stop you
from having to remember everything at once.
