The first thing Normandy teaches you is that the map is doing most of the work.
Hedgerows turn a two-kilometre advance into an afternoon, and the arrival
schedule decides the shape of the battle long before contact.

## What I got wrong

I spent the first two scenarios attacking on a broad front, because that is what
the counters in front of me suggested. It took a third attempt to notice that the
victory point locations were clustered along two roads, and that everything else
was decoration.

That is the moment the spreadsheet started. Not because the game hides the
information — it is all there — but because reading it a panel at a time meant I
kept forgetting half of it by the time I had found the other half.

## What the companion changed

With the victory locations and their values on one sheet, planning turned into a
five-minute job instead of a twenty-minute one. The orders of battle side by side
made the reinforcement timing obvious: the defender's problem is not strength, it
is that the strength arrives in the wrong order.

> Nothing here is a shortcut. It is the same information the game gives you,
> arranged so you can hold it in your head.

## Next

More scenarios, and a pass over the arrival table — the current sheet lists turns
but not entry hexes, which is the first thing I wanted to look up and could not.
